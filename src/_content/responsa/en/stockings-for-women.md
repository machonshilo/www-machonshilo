_Yom 4, 28-07-67 — 28 Tishre 5775 — 21-10-2014_
 
 **_Question_**
  
Shalom Rabbeinu:
 
1. Do you hold that dancing, clapping, etc. are forbidden on Shabbath and Yom Tov as per Beitza 30a?  
 
2. In Hil. Tefillah 3:1 the Rambam says that we can daven shaharith until hassoth.  Do you posken like him, or is the Kessef Mishnah there correct in saying that the Rambam meant that you can make up that davening by minha?  I'm not looking for a "heter for sleeping in", just trying to understand the parameters of tefillah.
 
3. Earlier you poskened that women don't have to wear stockings.  Why did you posken this why may I ask?  Why wouldn't the "hova" to wear them be an issue of makom sh'nahagu?
 
Kol tuv,
 
B
 
**_Response_**
 
Shalom Rav
 
 1.  Dancing, clapping (in the usual manner) etc. are asur on Shabath and Yom Ttov - see Rambam's MT Shabath 23:5. It should be noted that people holding hands and walking/shuffling around in a circle is not dancing and is mutar, as pointed out once by HaGaon HaRav Moshe Feinstein z'l. Clapping one hand on the back of the other is mutar. The Talmud (Bessa 30a and Shabath 148b) states that people do so anyway, and one need not chastise them; better that they do so unwittingly. 
 
2.  Yes, one may daven Shaharith until Hassoth. This is what the Mishna, the T. Y'rushalmi and Rambam (MT T'phila 3:1) state. According to Rambam, one must strive to daven by the end of the 4th hour, but if this did not work out, one may daven until Hassoth even if the delay was deliberate. According to the Mishna and Y'rushalmi the Halakha is according to the Hakhamim who do not distinguish between before and after the 4th hour, and it is therefore not required that one daven by 4 hours, even though it is clear that one should endeavour to daven earlier rather than later. The Keseph Mishna's initial interpretation of this Halakha is mistaken, as he himself points out quoting the Rashba and as he later wrote in the Shulhan 'Arukh OH 89:1. One may not daven Shaharith after Hassoth, as stated by the SA and stressed by Rama and Maghen Avraham.

3.  Rambam's opinion on the question of until when one can daven Shaharith has not been fully understood. I recently wrote about this subject (in Hebrew). Rambam's view is unique and very correct.

4.  Stockings or socks for women is apparently considered proper in certain communities, but I am not convinced that one can bestow the concept of minhagh hamaqom on such a practice. It is more accurate to describe it as a humra - a humra which I have never understood: stockings are designed to enhance the woman's looks and attractiveness, as any fashion designer will confirm. A part of a woman's leg that must be covered is to be covered by a skirt or dress (down to the bottom of the calf). A stocking or sock is no substitute, just as no-one would suggest that a woman could walk around dressed only in a body stocking.
 
Shalom 
 
Rabbi David Bar-Hayim
